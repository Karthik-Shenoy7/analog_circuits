1. The Common-Source (CS) Amplifier

The CS amplifier is the primary workhorse providing significant inverting voltage gain. Gate input draws virtually zero DC current (). In the presence of unbypassed source degeneration resistor , negative feedback stabilizes gain against process variations at the cost of reduced gain: .

2. The Common-Drain (CD) Source-Follower Stage

The Common-Drain stage takes input at the gate and output at the source. It features non-inverting unity voltage gain (), high input resistance, and very low output resistance , making it the quintessential low-impedance voltage buffer.

3. The Common-Gate (CG) Amplifier

The Common-Gate stage connects the AC ground to the gate. Input is applied to the source () and output is extracted from the drain (). It produces non-inverting voltage gain with wide bandwidth, serving as a current buffer or RF amplifier.
